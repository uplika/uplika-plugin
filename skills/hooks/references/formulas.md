# Hook formulas

Opening lines for the writing skills (`yt-script`, `threads`, `repurpose`, `schedule-week` and the channel skills). Pick a formula for the format, then fill it with the person's own specifics. Never fill it with a number, a result, a name or a date the person did not give you.

## YouTube

For the first seconds of a YouTube video. The opening has three jobs: confirm what the title promised, open a question the viewer cannot close yet, and show that the payoff exists. An opening that only does the last one is a summary.

| # | Formula | Shape | Example | Fails when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Statistic | A number the viewer did not know, stated flat, then the consequence. | 97% of the people who start a channel quit before video 30. Here is what the other 3% do differently. | Dies if the number is vague or unverifiable. Say the source out loud. |
| 2 | Someone Else's Result | A named person's outcome, then the mechanism. | This channel went from 400 subscribers to 200,000 in seven months on one format. I mapped it. | Do not invent the number. If you cannot name the channel, use a different formula. |
| 3 | The Mistake | Name the error the viewer is probably making right now. | Your first 30 seconds are why nobody finishes your videos. It is not the topic. | Only works if the mistake is common and the viewer can check it in one second. |
| 4 | Contrarian Flip | State the accepted advice, then reject it. | Everyone says post daily. I posted once a week for a year and tripled the channel. | Needs evidence in the next 15 seconds or it reads as contrarian for its own sake. |
| 5 | The Reveal | Promise a specific thing will be shown, then show it. | I am going to show you the exact prompt, on screen, that wrote this video. | You have to actually show it, early. A reveal at 8:00 is a retention cliff at 1:00. |
| 6 | The Superlative | The single best/worst/fastest of a category. | This is the fastest way to turn one long video into thirty Shorts. | Superlatives are cheap. Earn it by naming what it beat. |
| 7 | The Clock | Bound the payoff in time. | In the next six minutes you will have a working title, thumbnail and script for your next video. | The clock has to be true. Overrun it and the next video's hook is not believed. |
| 8 | I Tried It | First-person experiment with a stated cost. | I let an AI run my channel for 30 days. I did not touch the uploads. | Needs a real cost, such as time, money or risk, or there is no stake. |
| 9 | The Question | Ask the exact question the viewer typed into search. | Why do your videos die at 30 seconds when the topic is fine? | Must be the viewer's question, not yours. Pull it from your own comments. |
| 10 | Before and After | Two states, one cut between them. | This was my thumbnail. This is my thumbnail now. Same video, four times the click-through. | Only works with a visual. Do not use it on a talking-head-only video. |
| 11 | The Teardown | Take a real thing apart in public. | I pulled the nine most-viewed AI videos of the month and broke down what the titles share. | Name what you pulled and how many. A teardown of one example is an anecdote. |
| 12 | The Stack | Two named tools combined into one outcome. | Claude plus your YouTube Studio export gives you next week's upload schedule in one prompt. | Both tools must be nameable and the outcome must need both. |
| 13 | The Warning | A cost the viewer is about to pay. | Do not upload another video until you check this one setting. | If the cost is small the hook is a lie. Reserve it. |
| 14 | The List | A counted set, with the count in the first line. | Seven things I would do differently if I started a channel in 2026. | The count must be exact and every item must be different in kind. |
| 15 | The Receipt | Show the artifact first, explain second. | This is the analytics page. This video did 40% of the channel's watch time. Here is why. | Requires a real screenshot. A described receipt is not a receipt. |
| 16 | The Insider | Information from inside a system. | YouTube tells you which videos are underperforming. Almost nobody opens the report. | Do not claim insider access you do not have. |
| 17 | The Impossible Claim | State something that sounds untrue, then prove it. | You can write, title, thumbnail and schedule a week of videos without opening a single editor. | The proof has to start within 20 seconds or it reads as clickbait. |
| 18 | The Comparison | Two named options, one winner. | I wrote the same video three ways. One of them held 62% and the other two did not. | Say the losing option out loud. A comparison with no loser is an advert. |
| 19 | The Origin | Where a result actually came from. | Every video on this channel starts in the same 12-line file. Here it is. | Works once per channel. It is a reveal about you, and you only have one. |
| 20 | The Deadline | Something is changing on a date. | The old description format stops mattering this month. Here is what replaces it. | Only use with a real, checkable date. This is the fastest formula to lose trust with. |
| 21 | The Direct Address | Name the exact viewer in the first six words. | If you have under a thousand subscribers, this is the only video you need this week. | Narrow beats broad. 'If you make videos' addresses nobody. |

## Instagram

For Reels, and for any short vertical video. A Reel has two hooks at once: the first spoken line and the first text on screen. Write them separately; the on-screen one is shorter and blunter, because it is read before it is heard.

| # | Formula | Template | Example | On screen | Best for | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cost Confession | {Specific amount} is what {one mistake} cost me. | $18,000 is what one missing clause cost me. | $18,000 MISTAKE | Trust, fast. People finish the videos where you are the one who looks bad. | A fake cost. 'It cost me my peace of mind' is not a number and everybody can hear that. |
| 2 | Negative Command | Stop {common action}. Do {alternative} instead. | Stop posting three times a day. Post twice and reply to every comment for an hour. | STOP POSTING DAILY | Saves and arguments. The imperative gets attention that a statement does not. | Telling people to stop doing something nobody does. The habit has to be one the viewer recognises in themselves. |
| 3 | Nobody Tells You | Nobody tells you that {uncomfortable truth about the thing they want}. | Nobody tells you that your first 30 reels are supposed to flop. | YOUR FIRST 30 WILL FLOP | New audiences. It positions you as the person who says the quiet part. | Picking a truth everybody already says. If the comments are agreeing before you finish, it was not a secret. |
| 4 | The Replacement | {This thing} replaced {expensive thing} for {price}. | This $12 tool replaced the editor I was paying $2,000 a month. | $2,000 -> $12 | Demos and tool videos. The price gap does the work. | Overstating the replacement. If it replaced 60% of the job, say 60%, or the comments will say it for you. |
| 5 | Time Collapse | This used to take me {long time}. It now takes {short time}. | Proposals used to take me five hours. They take twenty minutes now. | 5 HOURS -> 20 MIN | Process videos where the payoff is speed. Reads instantly on screen. | A ratio nobody believes. 40 hours to 4 seconds gets treated as a lie even when it is true. |
| 6 | The Receipt | I {did thing} for {N days}. Here are the actual numbers. | I posted one reel a day for 60 days. Here is what it did to my DMs. | 60 DAYS. REAL NUMBERS. | Experiments. Pairs with a screen recording of the real dashboard, which is the whole value. | Not showing the screen. A numbers video with no screen recording is a numbers claim. |
| 7 | Wrong Way, Right Way | You are doing {thing} wrong, and it is not your fault. {The fix}. | You are writing captions wrong, and it is not your fault. Instagram hides everything after line one. | YOUR CAPTIONS ARE CUT | Teaching. The 'not your fault' half stops it reading as an insult, which is what kills this one. | Skipping the absolution. 'You are doing it wrong' on its own makes people leave rather than learn. |
| 8 | Insider Leak | I spent {N years} {inside the thing}. Here is what we never said out loud. | I spent six years selling agency retainers. Here is what we never put in the proposal. | 6 YEARS INSIDE | Authority without a credential slide. Works only if the years are real. | Promising a secret and delivering the brochure. The comments are merciless about this one. |
| 9 | The Steal | Steal this {artifact}. It took me {time} to get right. | Steal this four-line follow-up. It took me two years and a lot of dead leads. | STEAL THIS | Saves and sends, which are the two metrics that move reach the most. | The artifact not being on screen. If they cannot screenshot it, there is nothing to steal. |
| 10 | If This, Then Watch | If you {specific situation}, this {short time} fixes it. | If your reels get views and no followers, the next 40 seconds fix it. | VIEWS BUT NO FOLLOWERS? | Qualifying hard. Fewer people stay, and the ones who do are the ones who buy. | A situation so broad it qualifies nobody. 'If you want to grow' is not a situation. |
| 11 | Numbered With A Favourite | {N} {things} that {outcome}. Number {k} is the one nobody uses. | Five caption edits that double saves. Number four is the one nobody does. | 5 EDITS. #4 IS THE ONE. | Retention through the middle: naming a favourite gives people a reason to stay past three. | N above seven. Nobody believes you have nine good ones, and you do not. |
| 12 | The Objection | "{Objection they actually say}." Fine. Here is the version that works anyway. | "I don't want to be on camera." Fine. Here is the faceless version that still sells. | "I HATE BEING ON CAMERA" | Selling to the people who already said no once. | Inventing the objection. Pull it verbatim from a DM or a comment, including the bad grammar. |
| 13 | Before And After, On Screen | {Show the old thing}. {Show the new thing}. {One sentence on the lever}. | This was my profile in March. This is it now. I changed the name field, nothing else. | MARCH / NOW | Anything visual. The cut between the two frames is the hook, the words are secondary. | Three levers. Name one thing that changed or the transformation stops being believable. |
| 14 | The Callout | {Specific group}, this is the one you have been ignoring. | Photographers charging under $2,000: this is the pricing page you have been avoiding. | PHOTOGRAPHERS UNDER $2K | Cutting a broad audience down to the one that converts. Send rate goes up because people tag each other. | A group so large it is not a callout. 'Entrepreneurs' is not a group. |
| 15 | The Flop Record | My first {N} {attempts} did nothing. Here is what changed on {N+1}. | My first 47 reels averaged 300 views. Number 48 did 400,000. One thing was different. | 47 FLOPS, THEN THIS | People at the start, which is most of the audience. High comment rate. | Making the change sound like luck. If the answer is 'I got lucky', there is no video. |
| 16 | The Verbatim Question | "{Question exactly as it was asked}". I get this every week. | "How do I post consistently without hating it?" I get this one every week. | ASKED EVERY WEEK | Search. A question typed the way people type it is what Instagram search is matching against. | Tidying the question up. The value is that it is in their words, typos and all. |
| 17 | Head To Head | {A} versus {B}. I ran both for {time} and one of them was not close. | Carousels versus reels. I ran both for 90 days and it was not close. | CAROUSELS vs REELS | Comments. People arrive already holding an opinion, which is the point. | Refusing to pick. A comparison with a diplomatic ending is a video with no ending. |
| 18 | Cold Open Demo | {Start mid-action, already doing the thing.} Watch what this does. | Watch what happens when I paste a competitor's caption into this. | WATCH THIS | Screen recordings and anything with a visible result. No setup at all, the hand is already moving. | A result that takes twelve seconds to arrive. If the payoff is slow, cut to it and show the wait afterwards. |
| 19 | The Deadline | {Thing} changes on {date}. Do {action} before then. | Your top nine grid crops change the second you post a reel. Fix the covers first. | BEFORE YOU POST AGAIN | Urgency that is real. Platform changes, seasonal windows, policy updates. | Manufactured urgency. One fake deadline and every future one is discounted. |
| 20 | Permission | You do not need {the thing everyone says you need}. | You do not need a niche. You need one person you can name. | YOU DON'T NEED A NICHE | Relief. Very high save rate, because it removes a blocker rather than adding a task. | Removing the blocker and putting nothing in its place. The second half is the whole video. |
| 21 | Mid-Sentence Start | {...and that is when {the turn}.} | ...and that is when the client asked for the money back. Three days after approving it. | HE ASKED FOR A REFUND | Stories. Starting mid-scene makes the viewer feel they arrived late, which is a reason to stay. | Never going back to fill in the gap. Deliver the setup by second eight or it reads as a mistake. |
| 22 | Contrarian Flip | {Well-known saying, inverted.} | Nice guys finish first. | NICE GUYS FINISH FIRST | Comments. The viewer has to stop and check whether they misheard you. | Flipping a saying nobody says. The original has to be a reflex, or the inversion is just a sentence. |
| 23 | The Statistic | {N}% of {group} {surprising fact}. | 33% of high school graduates never read another book. | 33% NEVER READ AGAIN | Sends. A number a person can repeat at dinner gets sent to somebody. | A statistic you cannot source. Say where it came from in the video or do not use it. |
| 24 | The Reveal | This is the new {thing}. | This is the new Sony point and shoot, and it fixes the one thing I hated. | THE NEW ONE | Anything physical or visual. The object is the hook and the words are captions on it. | Holding the thing up without saying what it changes. A reveal with no stake is an advert. |
| 25 | Someone Else's Result | There was one {person} who {outcome} in {time}. | There was one creator who grew a million subscribers in a single month. | 1M SUBS IN ONE MONTH | Proof when the user does not have their own number yet. Third-person is allowed if it is true. | No name and no link. An anonymous miracle is a rumour. |
| 26 | The Superlative | The {fastest / biggest / only} {thing} is {the answer}. | The fastest way to get confident is to build evidence you cannot argue with. | THE FASTEST WAY | One-claim videos with no story attached. It is the most common shape in the wild and the easiest to write badly. | A superlative with a soft answer. If the second half is 'consistency', you have said nothing and the comments will tell you. |

## Short-form

For text posts, Threads posts and chains, carousel covers, captions and short videos, grouped by the reaction each family relies on. Pair one formula that stops the scroll (negation, specificity, question) with one that keeps people reading (story, confession, emotional). The Platform column is the original author's tag; some of those platforms are not Uplika channels, and the shape still works on the channels that are. The examples are the original author's illustrations, not facts: never reuse their numbers or their health and money claims.

### 10 Personal Confession

Written for Uplika. These are not the lists above translated: a translated English hook reads as an advert in Korean. Use them when the person writes in Korean, fill the brackets with their own specifics, and keep the endings their voice file uses.

Writing notes for Korean openings:

- Front-load the hook. Korean puts the verb last, so the words that carry the hook (the number, the reader, the keyword) go first.
- Address the reader by their situation (`이제 막 시작한 분`) rather than `당신`, which reads stiff or confrontational in most posts. Use `여러분` sparingly.
- Avoid translated phrasing such as `~에 대해 알아보겠습니다`, `이것이 바로 ~입니다` and `당신이 몰랐던 ~`. They read as translations or adverts.
- Cut intensifiers that promise without saying anything: `진짜`, `완전`, `대박`, `미쳤다`, `레전드`.
- Endings come from the voice file. Keep one register in a post (해요체, 합니다체 or 반말), never a mix.
- Write numbers the way people say and search them (`석 달`, `200만 원`), and only real ones.
- On Naver Blog the title follows the keyword set's `draftPrompt`; these patterns are for the opening lines of the body.
- On Threads, give the hook a line of its own and break the line after it.
