# Hooks

Shared reference for opening lines: the first seconds of a video, the first line of a post or chain, a carousel cover, the first line of a caption. The writing skills (`yt-script`, `threads`, `repurpose`, `schedule-week` and the channel skills) read it when they write an opening. It is not a task on its own and it publishes nothing.

The formulas are in `references/formulas.md`, with their sources and licenses at the top. The YouTube and Instagram lists, and some of the rules below, come from Jakeschincariol/youtube-agent-skill and Jakeschincariol/instagram-agent-skill (both MIT); the short-form list and the way of choosing below from rediumvex/viral-hooks-skill (MIT); the Korean patterns were written for Uplika.

| Writing | Section of `references/formulas.md` |

| The opening of a YouTube video | YouTube |
| A Reel, Short or other vertical video (spoken line and on-screen line) | Instagram |
| A text post, a Threads post or chain, a carousel cover, a caption's first line | Short-form |
| Anything in Korean | Korean patterns first; the other sections for the shape |

## How to use

1. Read the draft or the topic and who it is for. If there is only a topic, ask one question about the audience or the angle, then go on.
2. Choose three formulas from different families: one that stops the scroll (a negation, a number, a question) and one that keeps people reading (a story, a confession, an admission). The third is free.
3. Fill each one with the person's own specifics: their number, their reader, their situation. A hook that would fit anyone in the niche is not theirs.
4. For video, write the spoken line and the on-screen line separately. The on-screen line is shorter and blunter, because it is read before it is heard.
5. Keep a filled hook short enough to take in at a glance, and native to the channel: conversational on Threads and Instagram, searchable on YouTube.
6. Return the three with the formula name and section, a line on what each is best for, and which one to lead with and why. The writing skill shows them to the person with the rest of the draft.

## Rules

- Never invent a number, a result, a named person, a quote or a deadline. If the person cannot give a real one, pick a formula that does not need it.
- Specific beats big: the number, the time, the reader.
- One idea per hook. A hook carrying two promises keeps neither.
- Deliver what the hook promises early. A promise paid off late loses the people it brought in.
- Do not open with who the person is or with a request to follow or subscribe.
- Across a week of posts, vary the formulas. The same shape every day reads as automated.
- In Korean, write natively from the Korean patterns; do not translate an English hook word for word.
